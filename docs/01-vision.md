# Vision — the five pillars

Berean is a study tool *and* a ministry toolkit. The through-line is the Berean
ethos of Acts 17:11 — **eager study that examines the evidence.** Each pillar
below is a distinct, independently-useful capability; together they form a place
to study the Word and to serve those who teach it.

## 1. The Library — the whole text, every way in

- **Translations & versions.** Read and compare side-by-side. Public-domain
  translations are hosted directly (KJV, ASV, Webster, YLT, Darby, the World
  English Bible, Douay-Rheims, …); copyrighted modern translations (NIV, ESV,
  NASB, …) are accessed via licensed APIs with attribution, never redistributed
  (see [03-texts-and-licensing.md](03-texts-and-licensing.md)).
- **Original languages.** Hebrew (with cantillation where available), Aramaic,
  and Koine Greek, with **interlinear** alignment, **lexicons** (Strong's and
  others), morphology, and word-level study.
- **The wider canon & the "lost" books.** The apocrypha / deuterocanon (Tobit,
  Wisdom, Sirach, Maccabees, …) and the pseudepigrapha and non-canonical works
  (1 Enoch, Jubilees, the Gospel of Thomas, the Book of Jasher, …) — each labeled
  with **which tradition receives it and which does not**, so a reader sees the
  canon differences instead of being handed one canon silently.

## 2. The Evidence — so you can "see if these things were so"

- **Manuscripts:** the Dead Sea Scrolls, Codex Sinaiticus / Vaticanus /
  Alexandrinus, major papyri (𝔓⁵², …), the Masoretic tradition, the Septuagint.
- **Textual criticism:** variant readings shown at the verse level with witnesses,
  so a reader can see *where* manuscripts differ and *how* translators chose.
- **Archaeology & history:** the historical and archaeological context of people,
  places, and events — presented with citations and with honest labeling of what
  is established, contested, or a matter of faith rather than evidence.

**Principle:** every claim in this pillar is sourced. Berean's job is to *show the
evidence and its provenance*, not to adjudicate faith.

## 3. Study & Community

- Reading plans, topical study, word study, cross-references, and a concordance.
- Notes, highlights, and shareable study collections.
- A learning **community** where people study together, ask and answer, and
  contribute scholarship — in the Berean spirit: cite your sources, examine claims,
  and treat other traditions with respect.

## 4. Sermon Studio — for priests, pastors, and teachers

Build a sermon or lesson from a topic or a passage:
- gather the relevant passages, cross-references, and parallel accounts;
- pull original-language notes, lexicon entries, and public-domain commentary;
- surface historical/manuscript context;
- assemble an **outline**, key points, and illustrations;
- export to a clean handout / slides / manuscript.

Goal: compress hours of cross-referencing into a focused study, while keeping the
minister in full control of the message.

## 5. Church Operations

Open, self-hostable tools so a church can run its operations and care for its
community without depending on a closed platform:
- membership & households, small groups, and volunteer scheduling;
- events & calendar; communications (email/SMS) to groups;
- a sermon & teaching archive tied to the Library and Sermon Studio;
- (later, and carefully) giving/tithing via established, compliant integrations —
  **not** something Berean handles as raw funds itself.

This overlaps existing Church Management Systems (e.g. Planning Center); Berean's
distinctive is **open-source, self-hostable, and integrated with the study tools**.

## What Berean is not

- Not a single-denomination catechism — it presents traditions, it doesn't pick one.
- Not a replacement for a pastor, scholar, or the Church — it's a study *tool*.
- Not a redistributor of copyrighted translations — it respects those licenses.
