# Dedication & The Name

## To the Most High

This work is dedicated first to **God Most High** — *El Elyon* — *"the LORD, the
Most High over all the earth"* (Psalm 83:18), whom Melchizedek, priest of God Most
High, blessed as *"Maker of heaven and earth"* (Genesis 14:18–20). The Word this
project studies is not ours; we are only its students and stewards.

*Soli Deo gloria* — to God alone be the glory. May this tool serve the honest
study of His Word and be found faithful to it.

## The name — how "Berean" was chosen

When this repository needed a name, the goal was not just to *label* a Bible tool
but to name its *spirit*. Several names were weighed — **Scriptorium** (the hall
where monks copied and preserved Scripture) and simply **The Holy Bible** among
them. The name chosen was **Berean**, from Acts 17:10–12:

> *"As soon as it was night, the believers sent Paul and Silas away to Berea. On
> arriving there, they went to the Jewish synagogue. Now the Berean Jews were of
> more noble character than those in Thessalonica, for they **received the message
> with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see if what Paul
> said was true.** As a result, many of them believed."*

The Bereans were praised not for believing quickly, but for **searching the
Scriptures to see whether these things were so** — eager faith joined to diligent,
honest examination. That is precisely what this project is for: to study every
translation and the original tongues, to weigh the manuscript and historical
evidence, and to *examine* rather than merely repeat. The word carries the mission.

### A little history of the word

Berea (Greek *Beroea*, modern **Veria** in northern Greece) was a city in the
Roman province of Macedonia. Luke, the physician and careful chronicler, recorded
the episode in the Acts of the Apostles. The phrase rendered "of more noble
character" translates the Greek *eugenēs* — "well-born, noble" — here applied not
to birth but to a nobility of mind: an openness eager to receive truth *and* a
discipline to test it against Scripture. From this single account, "Berean" has
long stood, across Christian traditions, for the model student of the Word.

## In recognition — those who studied, preserved, and carried the Word

No list can be complete, and every name below stands for countless unnamed
others. We honor, across the generations and the traditions:

- **Within Scripture itself** — Ezra the scribe, who read the Law to the people
  and helped a nation remember it (Nehemiah 8); the prophets and apostles through
  whom it came; and Luke, who preserved the Bereans' example for us.
- **The preservers of the text** — the Jewish **scribes and Masoretes** of
  Tiberias, who guarded the Hebrew Scriptures letter by letter for centuries; the
  **Seventy** (Septuagint) translators who first carried the Hebrew into Greek; and
  the anonymous copyists of the great **scriptoria** who kept the text alive by hand.
- **The translators, often at great cost** — **Jerome**, whose Latin Vulgate
  served the Church for a millennium; **John Wycliffe** and the **Lollards**, who
  first gave the whole Bible to English readers and suffered for it; **Erasmus**,
  who published the Greek New Testament anew; **William Tyndale**, martyred in 1536
  for translating the Scriptures into common English, praying *"Lord, open the King
  of England's eyes"*; **Martin Luther**, whose German Bible reached a people; and
  the **translators of the King James Version** (1611) and the many faithful
  versions before and since.
- **The scholars of the evidence** — **Constantin von Tischendorf**, who recovered
  and preserved Codex Sinaiticus; the textual critics who assembled the great
  critical editions; and the scholars of the **Dead Sea Scrolls** and the
  manuscripts, who let us *see* how faithfully the text was handed down.
- **Those still carrying it to every tongue** — the Bible societies and the
  translators laboring to render Scripture into languages that have never had it,
  so that every people may read in their own words.
- **The living community** — every priest, pastor, teacher, scholar, and ordinary
  believer who opens the Word today to study it honestly — including those building
  and using this tool. You continue the Bereans' work.

We hold this recognition **across traditions** — Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox, and
Protestant alike have preserved and carried the Scriptures, often at great price.
Berean is built to honor that whole inheritance, and to hand it on.
